Trenchless sewer repair replaces or restores a damaged line without the extended excavation a traditional open-trench repair requires. For Walnut, CA properties where the sewer line runs beneath a driveway, mature landscaping, or hardscape, that difference often matters as much as the repair cost itself, since restoring dug-up concrete or established plantings adds its own expense on top of the plumbing work.

How It Works

Both trenchless methods restore or replace the interior of an existing sewer line with minimal disruption to the surface above it. Which one applies depends on the pipe's condition, diameter, and what the camera inspection finds.

Camera Inspection First

Before recommending trenchless repair, we run a camera through the line to confirm it's actually a good candidate for the method.

Pipe Lining

A resin-coated liner is inserted into the existing pipe and cured in place, creating a new pipe within the old one.

Pipe Bursting

A new pipe is pulled through the old line's path while a bursting head fractures the old pipe outward, useful when the existing line's diameter needs to increase.

Small Access Points

Both methods typically need only small excavation points rather than a trench along the pipe's full length.

When Trenchless Makes Sense (and When It Doesn't)

Good Candidate

  • Isolated cracks or root intrusion points along the line
  • Line runs under a driveway, patio, or established landscaping
  • Line's overall structure is otherwise sound

Not a Good Candidate

  • A fully collapsed section
  • Severe misalignment or bellying (sagging) along the run
  • Multiple, widely separated failure points

FAQs

Is trenchless repair more expensive than digging a trench?

It depends on the job. Material costs can run comparable to or higher than traditional repair, but avoiding the cost of restoring a driveway, patio, or mature landscaping often offsets that difference.

How long does trenchless sewer repair take?

Most trenchless jobs are completed within a single working day, though the exact overall timeline depends on line length and how many access points are actually needed.

Can trenchless repair fix a completely collapsed pipe?

Not usually. A fully collapsed section typically needs traditional excavation, which is exactly why a camera inspection happens before recommending either method.

Does trenchless repair actually last as long as a new pipe?

Both pipe lining and pipe bursting are designed to create a durable, long-lasting new pipe surface, but the right method depends on the existing line's condition, which we confirm before recommending one.
