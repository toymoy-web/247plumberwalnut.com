No hot water, a tank that's leaking at the base, or a pilot light that won't stay lit: water heater failures in Walnut, CA rarely wait for a convenient time. We repair most tank and tankless units the same visit and quote a replacement upfront when a repair isn't the better option.

Walnut's housing stock mostly dates from the 1970s-90s building boom, which puts a lot of original water heaters well past a typical 8-12 year tank lifespan. Walnut Valley Water District's Annual Water Quality Report classifies local water as moderately hard, and mineral buildup inside a tank is one of the most common reasons a water heater in this area fails earlier than its rated life, not just simple age. That's why we check for sediment buildup and element condition on any repair call, not just the symptom you noticed.

California Law: Is Your Water Heater Earthquake-Strapped?

California Health & Safety Code Section 19211 requires every residential tank water heater to be braced, anchored, or strapped to resist falling or shifting during an earthquake, with two strap points: one in the upper third of the tank and one in the lower third. It's a common miss on older installations, and we check it as part of any water heater visit.

Tank vs. Tankless

Tank Water Heater

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Simpler repair for most common failures
  • Needs more floor or closet space
  • Typical 8-12 year lifespan, shorter in hard-water areas without maintenance

Tankless Water Heater

  • Continuous hot water, no tank to run out
  • Smaller footprint, useful in tighter SGV garages and closets
  • Needs periodic descaling in hard-water areas like Walnut
  • Higher upfront cost, often lower long-term operating cost

FAQs

How long should a water heater last in Walnut, CA?

A well-maintained tank typically lasts 8-12 years, though local moderately hard water can shorten that without periodic flushing. Tankless units generally last longer with regular descaling.

Is it worth repairing an old water heater?

It depends on the tank's age and what failed. A minor part failure on a unit under 8 years old is usually worth repairing; a major failure on a 10+ year tank is usually more cost-effective to replace.

Do you check earthquake strapping on every visit?

Yes, since it's a real California code requirement and a common gap on older installations, we check it as part of any water heater service call.

Can you install a tankless unit in place of my old tank?

In most cases yes, though gas line sizing and venting sometimes need to be adjusted, which we'll walk through before quoting the job.
