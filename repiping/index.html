Walnut's housing stock mostly dates from the 1970s-90s building boom, which puts a lot of original copper supply lines at 30-50 years old. Whole-home repiping becomes worth considering once repeat leaks, persistently low pressure across multiple fixtures, or visibly corroded pipe start showing up together rather than as one isolated incident.

Walnut Valley Water District's Annual Water Quality Report classifies local water as moderately hard, and mineral buildup inside aging copper is one of the known contributors to internal pipe corrosion over decades, on top of ordinary age. That combination is why a 30-40 year old Walnut home with recurring pinhole leaks is often better served by a full repipe than another round of spot repairs on the same aging system.

Signs It Might Be Time to Repipe

Rather than reacting to a single leak, it's worth stepping back and looking at the pattern across the whole house. The signs below, especially in combination, are what actually point toward a system-wide issue rather than an isolated repair.

Repeat Leaks in Different Spots

One leak is a repair; a second or third unrelated leak within a year or two often means the whole system is reaching the end of its life.

Low Pressure at Multiple Fixtures

Pressure loss isolated to one fixture is usually local; a drop across the whole house points to a systemic pipe issue.

Discolored Water

Rust-colored water, especially right after the water's been off, can indicate internal pipe corrosion.

Visible Corrosion

Green or blue-tinted buildup on exposed copper fittings is a visible sign of ongoing corrosion.

Copper vs. PEX

Copper

  • Long track record, rigid and durable
  • Higher material and labor cost
  • Can still corrode over decades in hard-water areas

PEX

  • Flexible, fewer joints, faster installation
  • Resistant to the mineral-related corrosion that affects aging copper
  • Lower material cost, often lower total project cost

FAQs

How do I know if I need a full repipe or just another repair?

A single isolated leak is usually just a repair. Recurring leaks in different spots, house-wide low pressure, or visible corrosion together suggest the whole system may be nearing the end of its life.

Is PEX as durable as copper?

PEX has a strong track record for durability and is generally more resistant to the mineral-related corrosion that affects aging copper in hard-water areas, though copper remains a proven, long-standing option too.

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

It varies by home size and layout, but most whole-home repipes take a few days, including wall access, piping, and patching.

Will repiping fix low water pressure?

If the low pressure is caused by corroded or restricted pipe, yes. If it's caused by something else, like a municipal supply issue, a repipe won't address that separately.
