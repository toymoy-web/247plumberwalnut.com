Sump pumps are not standard equipment in Walnut. Most homes here have no basement, and the dry climate means many owners never think about one. They do turn up in specific places: hillside lots where drains collect water at a low point, homes with a below-grade garage or lower-level room, crawlspaces and yard drains that end in a pit, and some commercial buildings with underground parking.

When one of those pumps fails during the first big storm of the season, the water has nowhere else to go. If you have a pump you rarely think about, the best time to have it checked is before the rainy months, not during them. If you do not have one, we will tell you plainly whether your lot needs it.

Signs Your Sump Pump Needs Attention

Runs Constantly or Never Runs

A pump that cycles nonstop may have a stuck float switch or a failed check valve. One that never starts when the pit is full may have lost power or burned out.

Grinding or Rattling

Loud noises often point to a worn impeller or a motor working against debris in the pit.

Rust and Old Age

An older unit with rust or heavy mineral buildup is a candidate for replacement before it fails in a storm.

Water Rising in the Pit

Water that climbs toward the top of the pit after rain, or a damp smell nearby, means the pump is not keeping up.

Sump Pump or Sewage Ejector? They Are Not the Same

Sump Pump

  • Moves groundwater or rainwater out of a pit
  • Discharges outside or to an approved drain
  • Common for drainage pits, crawlspaces, and sunken garages
  • Not designed to handle sewage

Sewage Ejector Pump

  • Moves wastewater from fixtures below the main sewer line
  • Works in a sealed, vented basin
  • Needed for lower-level bathrooms or converted garage fixtures
  • Typically needs a permit when newly installed

If Your Pit Is Full Right Now

Stay Out of the Water

If the pit or floor is flooded near outlets or the pump cord, do not touch the pump or wade in. Shut off power at the breaker only if you can reach it without standing in water.

Check the Simple Things

If the area is dry enough to approach safely, confirm the pump is plugged in, the GFCI outlet has not tripped, and the float can move freely.

Move Belongings Up

Lift stored items and electronics off the floor while you wait.

FAQs

Do I need a sump pump in Walnut?

Many Walnut homes do not. You are more likely to need one with a below-grade room, a sunken garage, a crawlspace that collects water, or a yard drain system that ends in a pit. We check the lot and tell you plainly if a pump is not needed.

How often should a sump pump be tested?

At least once before the rainy season. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and confirm the pump starts, empties it, and shuts off on its own.

What is the difference between a sump pump and a sewage ejector?

A sump pump moves groundwater or rainwater. A sewage ejector moves wastewater from fixtures that sit below the main sewer line, in a sealed and vented basin. They are not interchangeable.

Can you install a battery backup?

Yes. A battery backup keeps the pump running during a power outage, which is when storms often cause the most trouble.

Do you service commercial sump systems?

Yes. Commercial properties with below-grade parking or mechanical rooms often have sump or ejector pumps, and we service those along with the building's other plumbing.
