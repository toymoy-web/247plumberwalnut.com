A disposal that hums but won't spin, leaks from underneath the sink, or has simply gone silent is one of the more common kitchen calls we get in Walnut, CA. Most jams and leaks are fixable the same visit; a motor failure or a unit old enough to have worn seals usually points toward replacement instead.

Common Disposal Problems

Humming, Not Spinning

Usually a jam that can be cleared, though a tripped internal reset switch or failed motor can cause the same sound.

Leaking From the Bottom

Often a worn seal or a cracked housing, both of which usually mean replacement rather than repair.

Won't Turn On At All

Check the reset button first; if that doesn't fix it, the motor or the electrical connection needs inspection.

Unusual Noise or Vibration

Can mean something has lodged in the grinding chamber, or that internal components have started to wear.

Repair vs. Replace

Repair

  • Clearing a jam or resetting the unit is usually same-visit
  • Lower cost when the motor and housing are still sound
  • Doesn't address an aging unit likely to fail again soon

Replace

  • Makes sense once leaks or motor issues start recurring
  • Newer units run quieter and handle more volume
  • Includes removal of the old unit and disposal

A disposal that's leaking onto cabinetry can cause secondary water damage the longer it's left; we'll flag that separately from the disposal repair itself if we see it.

A disposal that's due for replacement is also a reasonable time to reconsider horsepower. A unit that's undersized for a household's actual use tends to jam more often, which shows up as repeat service calls rather than a single clear failure. It's also worth knowing what shouldn't go down a disposal in the first place: fibrous vegetable scraps, coffee grounds in large volume, and grease all contribute to jams and premature wear even on a well-maintained unit. None of that means avoiding the disposal entirely, just being mindful of what consistently causes the same call to repeat.

Installation for a first-time disposal, where a sink previously had none, is a slightly different job than a swap: it usually involves confirming the sink has an available electrical outlet nearby and that the drain configuration can accommodate the unit, both of which we check before quoting the work.

FAQs

Why does my disposal hum but not spin?

That's usually a jam in the grinding chamber, which can often be cleared. If clearing it doesn't help, the issue may be the motor or an internal reset switch.

Is it worth repairing a leaking disposal?

A leak from a worn seal or cracked housing usually means replacement is more practical than repair, since those components aren't typically serviceable individually.

How long do garbage disposals usually last?

Most units last 8-12 years with normal use; leaks, recurring jams, or motor noise are signs a unit is nearing the end of its life.

Can you install a disposal where there wasn't one before?

Yes, as long as the sink and electrical setup can accommodate one; we'll check that during the visit.
