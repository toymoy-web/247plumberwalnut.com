Backflow prevention devices stop contaminated water from flowing backward into the clean water supply, and California's cross-connection control regulations (Title 17, California Code of Regulations) require most commercial properties and many residential irrigation systems to have one tested annually. It's an easy compliance item to lose track of between annual notices.

Who Typically Needs Testing

Backflow requirements vary by property type and by which water purveyor serves the address, but the categories below cover the situations we see most often across Walnut and the surrounding area.

Commercial Properties

Most commercial buildings in Walnut have a backflow device requiring annual testing and certification.

Irrigation Systems

Many residential and commercial irrigation systems have a dedicated backflow preventer that also needs periodic testing.

Fire Sprinkler Systems

Fire suppression systems connected to the water supply typically require their own backflow device and testing schedule.

New Construction

New backflow device installations need initial testing before being placed into service.

The Testing Process

A certified tester connects gauges to the backflow device and checks that its internal check valves and relief mechanism are functioning within the required specifications. Results are reported to your water purveyor to satisfy the annual compliance requirement, and we provide you with documentation for your own records as well.

The test itself typically takes well under an hour per device and doesn't require shutting off water to the rest of the property in most cases, so it can usually be scheduled without significantly disrupting a business's operating hours or a household's routine.

Getting ahead of the annual notice, rather than waiting for a violation letter from your water purveyor, keeps the property in good standing without the added pressure of a compliance deadline hanging over the next inspection cycle.

FAQs

How often does a backflow device need to be tested?

Most California water purveyors require annual testing for commercial properties and irrigation backflow devices, though your specific requirement is set by your local water agency.

What happens if my backflow device fails the test?

We can typically repair or replace a failed device as part of the same visit, then retest and submit documentation, minimizing the delay before your property is compliant again.

Do residential homes need backflow testing?

Not typically for the main water line, but a residential irrigation system with its own backflow preventer usually does, depending on your local water agency's requirements.

Who receives the test results?

We report results to your water purveyor to satisfy the compliance requirement and provide you with a copy for your own records.

Can you test multiple properties on the same visit?

Yes, for property managers with several locations, we can coordinate testing across multiple properties in a way that fits your schedule rather than treating each one as a separate request.
