A slab leak happens when a water line running beneath a home's concrete foundation develops a leak, and because it's under the slab, it rarely announces itself the way a visible pipe leak would. In Walnut, CA, where walnut's housing stock mostly dates from the 1970s-90s building boom, original copper supply lines are old enough that a slab leak is a realistic, not theoretical, possibility on a home that's never had one before.

Why Slab Leaks Happen Here

Two local factors compound each other: Walnut Valley Water District's Annual Water Quality Report classifies local water as moderately hard, and mineral-heavy water is a known contributor to internal pipe corrosion over decades. Combined with supply lines now 30-50 years old, that's why we specifically check for slab leak risk on older-home visits, not just react to a reported symptom.

Signs of a Slab Leak

Warm Spot on the Floor

Especially near a water heater or a hallway where a hot-water line runs beneath the slab.

Rising Water Bill

A bill that climbs with no change in household water use, confirmed by a moving meter with everything off.

Sound of Running Water

Audible water movement when no fixture is in use is a strong indicator of an active leak somewhere in the line.

Cracks in Flooring or Baseboards

Sustained moisture beneath a slab can eventually show up as flooring damage or baseboard separation.

If You Suspect an Active Slab Leak

Shut off the main valveIf the meter shows continuous movement with everything off, shutting off the main valve stops further loss while you schedule a visit.
Don't cut into the slab yourselfOpening concrete before the leak is located usually means opening the wrong spot.
Photograph any visible damageUseful documentation if you end up filing an insurance claim.

Repair Options

Slab Penetration

  • Opens the specific confirmed section of concrete to access the pipe directly
  • Straightforward for a single, well-located leak
  • Requires patching the slab and flooring afterward

Reroute (Above-Slab)

  • Reroutes the line through walls or attic space instead of cutting concrete
  • Often faster with less flooring disruption
  • Can be the better option when a line has multiple weak points

A slab leak diagnosis is only as good as the equipment behind it. Acoustic detection listens for the distinct sound of pressurized water escaping through a pinhole, while a pressure test isolates sections of the line to narrow down which run is actually affected. Used together, they typically narrow a leak to within a small, specific area of slab rather than an entire room, which is what keeps the repair itself from turning into a larger project than it needs to be.

FAQs

How do you find a slab leak without breaking up the whole floor?

Electronic leak detection uses acoustic sensors and pressure testing to narrow the leak to a specific point before any concrete is opened.

Is a reroute better than repairing the slab leak directly?

It depends on the line's condition. A single, well-located leak in an otherwise sound line often makes direct repair the simpler choice; a line with multiple weak points is often better rerouted.

How much water can a slab leak waste before it's noticed?

It varies widely, but a moving meter with everything off, or a bill that climbs with no usage change, are the clearest early signs before it becomes a larger loss.

Can a slab leak damage my foundation?

Prolonged, unaddressed moisture under a slab can eventually affect flooring and, in more severe cases, subgrade soil, which is why early detection matters.
