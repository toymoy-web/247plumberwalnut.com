Established tree roots are still one of the most common reasons a main sewer line develops a recurring backup, since roots seek out the moisture inside an aging clay or cast-iron line and gradually work their way in through joints and small cracks.

Signs Your Main Sewer Line Needs Attention

Multiple Fixtures Backing Up

If more than one drain backs up at the same time, especially the lowest fixture in the house, the problem is likely in the main line, not an individual fixture.

Recurring Clogs After Snaking

A main line that clogs again within weeks of being snaked often has root intrusion or a structural issue further down.

Sewage Smell in the Yard

Can indicate a cracked lateral line leaking beneath the yard, especially near mature trees.

Soggy or Sunken Yard Patches

An unexplained soft or sunken area in the yard, particularly along the line's likely path, can point to a collapsed or leaking section.

If Sewage Is Actively Backing Up

Avoid the affected areaSewage backup carries contamination risk; keep children and pets away from the affected area until it's cleaned up.

Diagnosing Before Digging

We run a camera through the line before recommending a repair method, so the scope reflects what's actually wrong rather than a general assumption. A camera inspection distinguishes between an isolated root intrusion point, a bellied (sagging) section, and a fully collapsed line, each of which calls for a different repair approach and a different price.

Repair Options

Traditional Open-Trench Repair

  • Direct access to the damaged section
  • Straightforward for a single, well-located problem
  • Requires digging up landscaping or hardscape above the line

Trenchless Repair

  • Repairs or replaces the line with minimal excavation
  • Better suited to lines running under driveways or mature landscaping
  • Not always suitable for a fully collapsed section

FAQs

How do I know if it's a sewer line problem and not just a clogged drain?

If more than one fixture backs up at once, especially the lowest drain in the house, or a single drain keeps clogging shortly after being snaked, that usually points to the main line rather than an isolated fixture clog.

Do tree roots really get into sewer lines?

Yes. Roots are drawn to the moisture inside an aging line and can work their way in through small cracks or joint gaps, especially in clay or older cast-iron pipe.

Is trenchless repair always an option?

Not always. It works well for many root-intrusion and crack repairs but isn't always suitable for a fully collapsed section, which a camera inspection will confirm before quoting either method.

Will my homeowner's insurance cover a sewer line repair?

Usually not for gradual wear like root intrusion, since that's considered maintenance rather than sudden damage. If a backup already caused water damage inside the home, that portion may be handled separately.
