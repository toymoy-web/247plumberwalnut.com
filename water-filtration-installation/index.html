Water filtration and water softening solve different problems, and it's worth understanding the distinction before choosing one. A softener addresses hardness minerals specifically; filtration targets taste, odor, sediment, and other compounds, which is a separate concern some Walnut, CA households want addressed alongside or instead of softening.

Whole-Home vs. Under-Sink Filtration

Whole-Home System

  • Filters water at the point it enters the home
  • Improves water quality at every fixture, including showers
  • Higher upfront cost, less frequent filter changes

Under-Sink System

  • Targets drinking and cooking water at a specific fixture
  • Lower upfront cost
  • More frequent filter cartridge changes

What Filtration Addresses

Filtration systems use different media depending on what's actually affecting your water, so the right setup depends on the specific issue rather than a one-size-fits-all cartridge.

Taste & Odor

Chlorine and other treatment byproducts can affect how tap water tastes and smells, even when it's safe to drink.

Sediment

Fine particulate that makes it through municipal treatment can be filtered out before it reaches your fixtures.

Specific Compounds

Different filter media target different contaminants; we'll match the system to what actually matters for your household.

Combined with Softening

Filtration and softening can be installed together for households wanting both hardness and taste/quality addressed.

FAQs

Is my Walnut, CA tap water actually safe to drink?

Yes. Walnut Valley Water District's Annual Water Quality Report confirms local water meets all state and federal safety standards. Filtration is about taste and preference, not correcting a safety problem.

Do I need both a softener and a filtration system?

Not necessarily. They address different things: a softener removes hardness minerals, while filtration targets taste, odor, and sediment. Some households want both installed together, others just one specifically.

How often do filter cartridges need to be changed?

It varies by system and household water use, but under-sink cartridges typically need changing every 6-12 months, while whole-home systems vary based on the media used.

Can filtration remove chlorine taste from tap water?

Yes, that's one of the most common reasons households choose filtration, since it directly addresses the taste and smell chlorine treatment can leave behind.

Does filtration reduce water pressure?

A well-sized system shouldn't cause a noticeable pressure drop; we size the unit to your household's actual flow needs specifically to avoid that.
