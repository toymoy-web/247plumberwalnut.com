A bathroom remodel almost always involves more plumbing work than it first appears, especially in Walnut homes built during the 1970s-90s boom, where original bathroom footprints tend to be smaller than what current fixture styles and layouts assume. Moving a toilet, widening a shower, or adding a second sink usually means relocating supply and drain lines, not just swapping fixtures in place.

What Bathroom Remodel Plumbing Involves

Fixture Relocation

Moving a toilet, sink, or shower drain to a new position within the room requires rerouting supply and waste lines beneath the floor or through walls.

Shower & Tub Conversion

Converting a tub to a walk-in shower, or the reverse, changes drain placement and often the water line configuration.

New Fixture Rough-In

Adding a second sink or a standalone tub where none existed means running entirely new supply and drain lines.

Updated Shutoffs & Valves

Older shutoff valves are often replaced during a remodel since they're easier to access with walls already open.

Working With Your Contractor or Designer

Most bathroom remodels involve a general contractor or designer coordinating multiple trades. We work directly with whoever's running the project, scheduling the rough-in and final fixture connection around the drywall, tile, and flooring timeline rather than requiring you to relay information between trades yourself.

Rough-In vs. Fixture Installation

Rough-In Phase

  • Supply and drain line placement before walls close up
  • Coordinated with the framing and drywall schedule
  • Includes shutoff valve placement

Final Fixture Installation

  • Connecting toilets, sinks, and shower/tub fixtures once tile and flooring are complete
  • Testing all connections before the project is signed off
  • Upfront price for the full scope before work begins

What Affects the Price

Scope of relocationMoving a fixture a few inches costs less than relocating it across the room.
Fixture countAdding a second sink or a standalone tub adds rough-in work beyond a like-for-like swap.
Wall & floor accessSlab-on-grade construction, common in Walnut, sometimes requires breaking concrete to relocate drain lines.

A remodel's plumbing scope is easiest to price accurately once the design is set, since fixture placement drives most of the cost; call (909) 505-3225 once you have a layout in mind and we can walk through what it involves before you finalize plans with your contractor.

Permits are typically required for plumbing relocation work, and we handle that as part of the project rather than leaving it for you or your general contractor to coordinate separately. Getting the permit lined up early in the process helps avoid a scheduling delay once the rough-in phase is ready to start, and it also protects you if the work is ever reviewed during a future home sale.

Timing the plumbing rough-in correctly within a broader remodel schedule matters more than most homeowners expect: work happening out of sequence with tile, drywall, or flooring almost always costs more to fix later than it would have cost to coordinate properly the first time around.

FAQs

Do I need a permit to move plumbing during a bathroom remodel?

In most cases yes, particularly for relocating supply or drain lines. We handle the permit process as part of the project scope.

Can you work with my contractor's timeline?

Yes, we coordinate the rough-in and final fixture installation around your contractor's overall schedule rather than working independently of it.

Is it expensive to move a toilet to a different spot in the bathroom?

It depends on the distance and whether the drain line needs to cross the existing slab or floor joists; a few inches costs much less than relocating it across the room.

Can you convert my bathtub to a walk-in shower?

Yes, this is one of the more common remodel requests we see, and it typically involves drain relocation and sometimes a change to the water line configuration.
