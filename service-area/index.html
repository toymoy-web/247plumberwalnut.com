What Sets Each City Apart

Diamond Bar shares Walnut's own water utility and a similar 1980s-era construction profile, but adds its own hillside terrain considerations. Rowland Heights, an unincorporated community rather than a city, combines a dense commercial corridor with residential neighborhoods, meaning both restaurant-scale and household-scale plumbing needs. La Puente traces back to the same 1842 land grant as Walnut itself and sits on mostly flat terrain, a contrast to the hillside construction common elsewhere in our coverage area. Covina's housing dates predominantly from a 1950s postwar boom, noticeably older than Walnut's own 1970s-90s stock, meaning original galvanized supply lines are more common there. San Dimas shares Walnut's hillside terrain and a similar 1960 incorporation timeline, with its own separate water utility.
