West Covina sits just northwest of Walnut and shares a border with it, which makes it one of the first places a Walnut-based dispatch reaches. It is also far larger than Walnut, with 109,501 residents at the 2020 census, and most of that growth came in a single burst. The city went from 4,499 residents in 1950 to 50,645 in 1960, one of the fastest postwar growth rates in the country.

That boom matters for plumbing. A large share of West Covina's homes date from the 1950s and 1960s, roughly a generation before most of Walnut. Original galvanized supply lines, clay or cast iron sewer laterals, and first-generation water heaters are more likely to be near the end of their service life.

What a Postwar Building Boom Means for Plumbing

Original Galvanized Supply Lines

Homes from the 1950s and 1960s may still carry the galvanized steel supply piping they were built with. It corrodes from the inside, so low pressure and discolored water often show up long before a visible leak.

Older Sewer Laterals

Clay and cast iron laterals from that era are more prone to cracks, offset joints, and root intrusion. A recurring main-line backup here usually calls for a camera inspection instead of another round of snaking.

Retail and Restaurant Corridors

Plaza West Covina, Eastland Center, and The Heights at West Covina anchor a large commercial area. Busy restrooms, kitchens, and grease lines have very different plumbing demands than a house.

Mixed Housing Ages

Neighborhoods such as South Hills sit alongside the older tracts. We confirm what is actually behind the walls before quoting instead of assuming one pipe age for the whole city.

Repiping, sewer line diagnostics, and water heater replacement come up often here because of the housing age, along with drain cleaning, leak detection, and fixture repair. Around the malls and the West Covina Sportsplex we also handle backflow testing and hydro jetting for restaurant drain lines.
