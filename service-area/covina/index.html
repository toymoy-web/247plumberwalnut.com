Covina is the oldest incorporated city in our San Gabriel Valley coverage area by a wide margin, dating to 1901, with a preserved historic downtown built during the region's citrus industry boom. The city's real growth spike came later, though: Covina's population jumped from 3,956 in 1950 to 20,124 by 1960, a 409% increase, meaning a large share of the city's housing dates specifically from that postwar 1950s building boom, noticeably older than Walnut's own 1970s-90s stock.

What That Housing Age Means for Plumbing

Original Galvanized Supply Lines

1950s-era homes are more likely to still have original galvanized steel supply piping, which corrodes internally and restricts flow well before it visibly leaks.

Clay & Cast-Iron Sewer Laterals

Sewer lines from this era are more often clay or cast iron rather than modern PVC, both more vulnerable to root intrusion and joint failure.

Undersized Gas Lines for Modern Appliances

Homes from this period sometimes need gas line upgrades to accommodate modern tankless water heaters or larger appliances.

Historic Downtown Commercial Buildings

Covina's preserved downtown core includes older commercial buildings with their own aging plumbing infrastructure needs.
