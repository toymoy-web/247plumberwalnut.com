Suzanne Park at 625 Suzanne Road is the first park the City of Walnut built. It opened in 1961 as Walnut City Park on former orange grove land and took the name Suzanne Park in the late 1960s, according to the city. The park covers about 14 acres and is one of four that residents can reserve.

Walnut incorporated in 1959, so the park dates to the city's earliest years. The name also appears on Suzanne Middle School in the Walnut Valley Unified School District, which is part of why residents use it to say where they live.

Yards, Trees and Drains

The points below are general plumbing information for any lot with mature landscaping, not findings about this area.

Roots and sewer lines

Tree roots can enter a sewer lateral at a joint and cause repeat backups. A camera inspection shows whether roots are the cause before anyone digs.

Clogs that keep returning

A line that clears and slows again within weeks usually has a cause beyond a simple blockage, such as grease buildup, a belly in the pipe or roots.

Irrigation and backflow

Sprinkler systems connect to the household water supply and many require a backflow device that is tested on a schedule. Testing protects the drinking water from irrigation water.

Outdoor hose bibs and shutoffs

A dripping hose bib or a stiff outdoor shutoff is a small problem that becomes a wet wall if ignored. They are worth checking before the seasons change.

Suzanne Park Plumbing FAQs

Where is Suzanne Park in Walnut?

Suzanne Park is at 625 Suzanne Road in Walnut. It opened in 1961 as the city's first park, and the city does not publish a boundary for the surrounding neighborhood.

How old are the homes near Suzanne Park?

We found no documented build dates for the streets around the park. Citywide listing data shows a median build year of 1983, but that is a citywide figure and not a statement about this area.

Do tree roots always cause sewer backups?

No. Roots are one possible cause among several, including grease, a sagging section of pipe and a damaged joint. A camera inspection shows which one you have.

Do sprinkler systems need backflow testing?

Many irrigation systems have a backflow device that must be tested on a schedule set by the local water provider. Call to ask about testing for your address.
