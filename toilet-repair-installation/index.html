Common Toilet Problems

Toilets fail in a handful of predictable ways, and knowing which one you're dealing with helps set expectations before a technician arrives. Most calls fall into one of the four categories below, though it's common for more than one to show up at once on an older unit that hasn't been serviced in years.

Running Constantly

Usually a worn flapper or fill valve, but can also mean a cracked overflow tube that's harder to spot without opening the tank.

Rocking at the Base

Often a failed wax ring or a cracked flange, both of which can let water leak beneath the floor if left alone.

Repeated Clogging

Can point to a partial line blockage further down rather than the toilet itself, especially if it's a recent pattern.

Water Around the Base

Usually a wax ring failure, and one of the more common reasons a subfloor gets water damage without an obvious leak upstairs.

Repair vs. Full Replacement

Repair

  • Fill valve, flapper, and fill-tube fixes are usually same-visit
  • Lower cost for an isolated failure
  • Doesn't address an aging bowl or an outdated high-flow model

Full Replacement

  • Makes sense once repairs start recurring on the same unit
  • Newer low-flow models reduce water use
  • Includes wax ring, flange check, and supply line replacement
  • Upfront price before work starts

Choosing a Replacement

Modern low-flow toilets use roughly a third of the water older 1970s-90s era units do, which is worth factoring in if you're replacing rather than repairing. Comfort-height bowls sit a couple inches taller than standard and are worth considering for households where that matters. We'll walk through the realistic options for your bathroom's rough-in spacing before you commit to a model.

FAQs

Why does my toilet keep running after I jiggle the handle?

That's usually a worn flapper that isn't sealing fully, though a warped or corroded fill valve can cause the same symptom and needs a different fix.

Is a rocking toilet dangerous to ignore?

It won't cause sudden damage, but a failed wax ring underneath can let water reach the subfloor slowly, which gets more expensive the longer it's ignored.

Do you install comfort-height or specialty toilets?

Yes, we install standard, comfort-height, and most specialty bowl styles; let us know your preference when you call.

Can a toilet clog mean a bigger sewer line problem?

It can, especially if it's a new pattern or affects more than one fixture. A camera inspection can confirm before assuming it's isolated to the toilet.
